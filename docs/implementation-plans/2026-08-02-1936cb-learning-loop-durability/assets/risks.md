# Risks

| ID | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Steps |
|----|------|------------|--------|------------|-------|
| RISK-1 | State, rationale, capture, and feedback are model/operator-authored; valid structure can still contain semantic injection or forged wrapper markers. | Medium | High | Typed script writes; one resolver read path; fresh wrappers and marker neutralization for every source including state/overflow; trusted-base scope gate; hostile stored-then-read tests. | 1.2, 2.3, 3.1, 4.1, 4.2, 5.1, 6.1, 6.2, 8.1 |
| RISK-2 | A non-blocking due enqueue, phase harvest, or source read can fail while the workflow appears successful. | Medium | High | Structured complete/empty/degraded status; no success wording on persistence failure; ranking blocked on incomplete expected sources; phase receipt retry; seeded degradation tests. | 3.2, 3.3, 4.1, 5.1, 5.2, 7.1 |
| RISK-3 | Concurrent/replayed writers, abandoned locks, or process death leave duplicates, orphan files, lost dispositions, or a consumed due without its run. | Medium | High | Content-addressed ids; 30-second locks; random atomic temps; write run/overflow before manifest/summary; immutable completed runs; explicit repair/quarantine; crash-point and concurrency matrix. | 1.1, 1.2, 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 3.1, 6.1, 7.1 |
| RISK-4 | Git cannot atomically update state and create/merge a PR; branch-local consumption could be invisible or duplicate proposals. | High | High | `origin/main` authority; deterministic correlation branches; pending until merged; open PR means in-flight/resume; proposal-pending reconciliation; state-only record PR for all-declined runs. | 5.2, 6.1, 6.2, 7.1, 9.1 |
| RISK-5 | Per-phase promotion becomes a noisy duplicate, redistills differently at finalization, or loses phase/REQ provenance. | Medium | Medium | Explicit typed candidate manifest; verified source-record ids; concern map; one batch write per category; immutable phase receipts replayed at finalization. | 3.1, 3.2, 3.3 |
| RISK-6 | Feedback/learning bounds silently truncate data, or a two-file overflow update loses entries on interruption. | Medium | High | Exact post-sanitization byte bound with reject-before-write; append-only overflow-first batches; digest-bound summary; deterministic duplicate repair; sentinel/crash tests. | 2.1, 2.2, 2.3 |
| RISK-7 | State, ledger, feedback, and overflow grow without bound, increasing lock duration and model context cost. | Medium | High | Exact limits in `operational-limits.md`; sharded immutable runs; snapshot-bound paging; plus-one fail-before-mutation; one ledger rewrite per category; maximum-size focused runtime and remeasured suite evidence. | 1.1, 1.3, 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 3.1, 3.3, 4.1, 4.2, 8.2 |
| RISK-8 | Scripts/schemas work only while dogfooding or drift across plugin payloads, manifests, dogfood, catalogs, and consumer installs. | High | High | Single named owner per executable; explicit schema payload entries and byte-identity gates; sync in each owning step; foreign consumer execution; structural evals and generated-catalog gates. | 1.3, 2.1, 2.2, 3.2, 4.1, 4.2, 4.3, 5.1, 8.1, 8.2 |
| RISK-9 | An SI proposal weakens a guard/writer/resolver, hand-edits state, or adds commits after validation. | Medium | Critical | Pin trusted base by OID; render/verify state from trusted copies; merge/re-derive before push; confirm remote head; rerun enforcement in the expected-head merge process; positive and adversarial controls. | 6.2, 7.1, 9.1 |
| RISK-10 | Corrupt or newer-version state bricks the workflow, is silently downgraded, or loses audit provenance during repair. | Low | High | Status/validate surface; forward-version read-only refusal; explicit current-version migration; quarantine rather than deletion; provenance fields; orphan/temp inspection and recovery tests. | 1.1, 1.2, 1.3 |
